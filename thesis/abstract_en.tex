Vision Foundation Models (VFMs) such as DINOv2~\cite{dinov2} and CLIP~\cite{radford2021clip} extract powerful semantic features, but only at very low spatial resolution, which limits their use in dense prediction tasks. Feature upsamplers such as FeatUp~\cite{featup} and LoftUp~\cite{loftup} address this by recovering a high-resolution feature map from a low-resolution one without distorting what the features mean, and the state-of-the-art AnyUp~\cite{anyup} does so at any output resolution and for any encoder. AnyUp, however, was built for still images. Applied to video, it upsamples each frame on its own and ignores how frames relate over time, so its output flickers and object boundaries shift from frame to frame. This thesis proposes ``AnyUp3D,'' an extension of AnyUp that adds temporal consistency while keeping its ability to work with any backbone. It makes two changes to the upsampling pipeline: the encoder's convolutions become 3D, so that it reads short spans of time as well as space; and the cross-attention becomes 3D, letting each output pixel draw on nearby frames as well as nearby pixels when deciding its feature. AnyUp3D is trained on a subset of UCF-101 (1{,}170 clips, 10 action classes), starting with single frames and gradually increasing clip length. Training uses a four-part loss: reconstruction against the teacher encoder, agreement with the low-resolution input, stability under image perturbation, and a temporal term that penalises flicker but switches off when consecutive frames genuinely differ, so real motion is not suppressed. On DAVIS-2017, feature similarity between adjacent frames rises from $0.863$ to $0.944$ ($+0.081$) against the frame-by-frame 2D baseline, improving on all 30 clips. In a training-free label-propagation test with no learned segmentation head, the $\mathcal{J}\&\mathcal{F}$ score rises from $0.266$ to $0.347$ and AnyUp3D wins on 27 of 30 clips, even though it was applied without retraining to features from Video Swin Transformer, an encoder it never saw in training. This work contributes the first temporally consistent feature upsampler tied to neither a specific backbone nor a specific task, along with a reproducible training pipeline. These results come from only 6{,}000 training steps, after initializing the weights from the original AnyUp2D official weights.
